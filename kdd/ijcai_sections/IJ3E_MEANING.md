## 3.6 What this means for the field
This is the forecast combination puzzle \cite{claeskens2016forecast}, measured in a domain where it had not been:
weight estimation error, not a shortage of mechanisms, caps the adaptation lever.

**The bound is a function of the metric.** Every oracle here maximises Spearman, including the +0.0051 headline.
Under the metric §5 argues a campaign experiences — top-of-list recall — the transferable headroom is **+0.0130**
against **+0.0046**, a factor of 2.8, and the weights a Spearman objective selects capture **10.4%** of it. So
+0.0051 bounds Spearman at fixed pool and per-assay weights; it is not a claim that weighting is worthless under a
ranking objective, where the lever is three times larger.

**The rule that reaches it works at the threshold a campaign operates at.** Fit each assay's weights to maximise
top-of-list recall on half its variants, then shrink toward the panel constant. At top-1% this reaches **0.1239**
against **0.1146** for Spearman-selected weights: **+0.0093**, CI [+0.0014, +0.0171]. The advantage is not
detectable at 5% (+0.0029, CI [−0.0028, +0.0085]) or 10% (+0.0014), and on indels the same rule is the worst of the
four candidates — the same cause in both cases, since 5% of a 154-variant assay is eight variants and the counting
statistic is not estimable there. The estimability story of §3.4, applied to a noisier statistic.

**When the oracle is small, the pool is the problem**: the next +0.01 has to come from an expert that disagrees
usefully, which §4 builds. A pool built to disagree more could make gating worthwhile again.
